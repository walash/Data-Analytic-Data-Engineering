## Content

The global jobs gap is hiding in plain sight — and the data tells a striking story.

While the headline global unemployment rate sits at 5.0% (2024), the real picture is far more complex. The ILO estimates 402 million people worldwide want to work but can't — a number that rarely makes the news.

Here's what the data reveals across three regions:

🇸 United States (March 2026):
→ Overall unemployment: 4.3% — but teen unemployment hits 13.7%
→ Long-term unemployment (27+ weeks) rose by 322K over the past year to 1.8M
→ Black unemployment (7.1%) is nearly 2x the white rate (3.6%) — structural gaps persist

 Africa:
→ Continental average: ~7.0%, but South Africa sits at 32.9% (Q1 2025)
→ Youth unemployment in South Africa: 45.5% — nearly 1 in 2 young people
→ For every 11 young Africans entering the workforce, only 3 jobs are created
→ 80%+ of employment remains informal

 Global:
→ Youth unemployment: 12.6% globally — 173M young women classified as NEET
→ AI-exposed jobs are seeing skills change 66% faster than other roles (PwC, 2025)
→ The jobs gap rate: 8.2% in high-income countries vs. 20.5% in low-income nations

The AI disruption narrative is real but nuanced. Goldman Sachs projects a ~0.5% temporary US unemployment rise from AI transition — but the structural inequality in who bears that burden matters enormously.

Data analysts and workforce economists have a critical role to play in making these patterns visible and actionable.
