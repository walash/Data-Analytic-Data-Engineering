## Content

304 million people. That's how many individuals currently live outside their country of birth — and the data behind this number tells a story far more nuanced than most headlines suggest.

Here's what the latest 2024-2025 migration data reveals:

🌍 Global picture: The world hit a record 123.2M forcibly displaced people by end-2024 — driven by conflict in Sudan, Ukraine, and beyond. By mid-2025, that figure edged down to 117.3M, the first decline in over a decade. Fragile progress, but progress nonetheless.

🇺🇸 United States: The US hosts 52.4M international migrants — 17% of the global total. Yet FY2025 saw only 237,538 Southwest border apprehensions, the lowest since 1970. Net international migration dropped from 2.7M to 1.3M year-over-year. Policy shifts are reshaping labor supply in real time.

🌍 Africa: 64% of African migrants stay within the continent — a fact that challenges the dominant South-to-North migration narrative. With 29.2M international migrants and growing intra-regional mobility, Africa's migration story is fundamentally one of regional integration.

💸 The economic thread tying it all together? Remittances. $685 billion flowed to low- and middle-income countries in 2024 — surpassing foreign direct investment. For many families across Africa, Latin America, and Asia, this IS the development finance.

Data analytics is how we move beyond the politics and understand what's actually happening — and what's coming next with climate-driven displacement projected to accelerate.
