## Content

The global unemployment rate sits at 4.9% — near a historic low. But that headline number tells only half the story.

Dig deeper and the picture gets more complex:

🇺🇸 In the US, unemployment held at 4.3% in April 2026 — but teenage unemployment hit 14.4%, and the labor force participation rate dropped to 61.8%, its lowest since 2021. Federal government employment alone shed 348,000 jobs since late 2024.

🌍 In Sub-Saharan Africa, the headline youth unemployment rate is 8.9% — but South Africa's youth unemployment stands at a staggering 62.4%. Over 10 million young Africans enter the labor market each year competing for just ~3 million formal jobs. And 90% of employed African youth work in the informal sector.

🌐 Globally, 2.1 billion workers are in informal employment — meaning the "low" 4.9% rate masks a far deeper vulnerability. Add 257 million young people classified as NEET (Not in Employment, Education, or Training), and the structural challenge becomes clear.

Then there's AI. The WEF projected AI would displace 85 million jobs while creating 133 million new ones by 2025. We're seeing early signals: software developers (+17.9% projected growth by 2033), financial advisors (+17.1%) — but also declines in claims adjusters and administrative roles.

The labor market isn't broken — it's bifurcating. The gap between formal and informal, young and experienced, AI-augmented and AI-displaced is widening fast.

Data literacy, adaptability, and reskilling aren't optional anymore. They're the new baseline.
