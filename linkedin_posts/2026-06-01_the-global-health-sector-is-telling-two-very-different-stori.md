## Content

The global health sector is telling two very different stories — and the data is striking.

The US spends $15,474 per person on healthcare (18% of GDP) — nearly 2x the high-income country average. Yet life expectancy sits at ~77.5 years, trailing many peers who spend far less. More spending ≠ better outcomes.

Meanwhile, Africa's average life expectancy is 64.2 years — a 13-year gap vs. the US. But the real story is the variance: Tunisia reaches 77 years while Nigeria sits at 54.8. The difference? Health investment, governance, and infrastructure.

Globally, the picture gets more complex:
📊 Non-communicable diseases now cause 74% of all deaths worldwide — and that number is rising
🏥 The world faces a shortage of 11 million health workers by 2030
💻 The digital health market is exploding — $88B in the US alone, $483B globally

The opportunity? Africa's digital health sector is massively underinvested relative to its potential. AI, telehealth, and remote monitoring could leapfrog traditional infrastructure gaps — just as mobile banking did for financial inclusion.

The challenge? Workforce pipelines, equitable access, and bridging the data divide between high-income and low-income health systems.

Data analytics is at the center of solving this — from predictive disease modeling to health system optimization. The next decade of global health will be won or lost on data.
