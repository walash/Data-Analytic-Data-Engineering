## Content

The global energy map is being redrawn — and the data tells a fascinating story.
US crude oil production hit a record 13.6M barrels/day in 2025, up from 13.2M bpd in 2024. What's remarkable? This was achieved with FEWER wells. Horizontal drilling, AI-driven reservoir modelling, and real-time analytics are doing the heavy lifting — STEM roles now make up 18.5% of US oil & gas extraction jobs, nearly 3× the national average.
Meanwhile, Africa is at a pivotal inflection point. The continent holds ~532 trillion cubic feet of proven natural gas reserves, yet receives only ~2% of global clean-energy investment despite representing 20% of the world's population. Africa's total energy investment of $110B in 2024 is just 5% of global clean-energy spending — a structural funding gap that demands attention.
The Dangote Refinery in Nigeria (650,000 bpd nameplate, stress-tested to 700,000 bpd in June 2026) is a game-changer for African downstream. Combined with new FLNG projects in Senegal, Mauritania, and Congo, the continent is building the infrastructure to convert resources into real economic value.
Globally, the investment split is stark: $2.2 trillion went to clean energy vs. $1.1 trillion to fossil fuels in 2025 — a 2:1 ratio. Yet oil & gas companies directed only ~4% of their capex to clean energy. The transition is happening, but unevenly.
For data professionals: the sector's productivity gains are being driven by analytics. ~75% of major energy companies now use digital platforms, but only 15% describe AI as deeply integrated. The opportunity gap is enormous.
