## Content

Africa is quietly outgrowing the world  and the data is hard to ignore.

The 2026 economic outlook tells a fascinating story of divergence:

📊 GDP Growth Rates:
• Africa: 4.2% (AfDB) — leading all major regions
• Global average: 3.1% (IMF)
• United States: ~1.9% (OECD/Fed range)

💡 What's driving Africa's edge? A young, expanding workforce, rising domestic consumption, and accelerating digital investment. UNCTAD reports that greenfield digital investment in developing economies surged from $6B to $37B between 2020 and 2024 — much of it flowing into Africa's fastest-growing markets.

Meanwhile, the US economy is navigating real headwinds: the OECD projects growth slowing to 1.5% in 2026, weighed down by the highest effective tariff rate since the 1930s (19.5%) and tighter immigration-driven labor supply constraints. The Fed's own projection sits at 2.3% — but both scenarios signal a meaningful deceleration from 2025's resilience.

Globally, merchandise trade growth is forecast at just 0.5% for 2026 (WTO) — a sharp drop from 2.4% in 2025. Yet digital trade in services is holding firm at 4.4% growth, underscoring where the real economic momentum lies.

For data professionals, this divergence creates real opportunity. The regions growing fastest are also the ones most hungry for analytical talent to make sense of complex, fast-moving economic signals.

What trends are you watching in the global economy right now? The World Bank Group Goldman Sachs McKinsey & Company — would love your perspectives on where the growth story goes from here.
