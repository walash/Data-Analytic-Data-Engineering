## Content

304 million people. That's how many international migrants the world had in 2024 — nearly double the figure from 1990.

But here's what the headline number doesn't tell you: the story behind migration is increasingly one of crisis, resilience, and economic lifelines.

📊 Key data points shaping global migration in 2024–2025:

🌍 Global: 123.2 million people are forcibly displaced — 1 in every 67 people on Earth. Of these, 73.5M are internally displaced, 42.7M are refugees, and 8.4M are asylum-seekers. Children make up 40% of all displaced persons.

🇺🇸 United States: Hosts 52.4 million migrants — 17.2% of the global total — making it the world's #1 destination by a wide margin. A strong US labor market (foreign-born employment 11% above pre-pandemic levels) is fueling record remittance outflows globally.

🌍 Africa: Sudan alone has generated 14.3 million displaced people — the world's largest single displacement crisis. Yet remittances to Sub-Saharan Africa still grew 2.4% in 2024, proving migration's economic resilience even amid conflict.

💸 The economic angle: Global remittances hit $905 billion in 2024, with $685B flowing to low- and middle-income countries — surpassing both FDI and foreign aid. India leads at $129B received, followed by Mexico at $68B.

The data tells a dual story: migration as opportunity and migration as survival. For data professionals, this sector offers some of the most complex, high-impact analytical challenges — from displacement modeling to remittance corridor analysis.
