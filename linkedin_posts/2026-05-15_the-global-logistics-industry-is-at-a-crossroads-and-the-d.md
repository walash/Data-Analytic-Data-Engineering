## Content

The global logistics industry is at a crossroads — and the data tells a fascinating story of divergence. 🚛✈️🌍

The global logistics market hit $10.17 trillion in 2024 and is on track to reach $14.39 trillion by 2029. But beneath that headline number, regional realities couldn't be more different.

In the US, truck freight volumes are finally recovering — up ~1.6% in 2025 after two years of decline. EV adoption in transport is gaining ground too, with EVs projected to hit 11.8% of vehicle sales by 2026. Yet logistics costs remain relatively efficient at 8.7% of GDP.

Africa tells a different story. Cross-border road freight is growing at ~3.9% annually — outpacing the US — fueled by AfCFTA implementation and $19B+ in corridor investments like the Lobito Atlantic Railway and the Abidjan-Lagos Highway. The opportunity is enormous. But logistics costs at 14.6% of GDP (nearly 1.7x the US) reveal the infrastructure gap that still needs closing.

Globally, air cargo demand surged 3.4% in 2025, driven by e-commerce and tariff front-loading. That momentum is expected to moderate to 2.6% in 2026 as trade flows normalize.

Here's what I find most compelling: Africa's logistics cost gap isn't just a challenge — it's a $68-108B annual investment opportunity. Closing that gap to global averages could unlock billions in trade value and reshape supply chains across the continent.

Data analytics is at the heart of solving these inefficiencies — from route optimization to demand forecasting to infrastructure planning.
