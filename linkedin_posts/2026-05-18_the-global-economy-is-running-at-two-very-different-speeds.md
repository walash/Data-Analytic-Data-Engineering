## Content

The global economy is running at two very different speeds — and the data tells a fascinating story. 📊

Africa is projected to grow at 4.3% in 2026, nearly double the U.S. rate of 2.2% and well above the global average of 3.1%. In fact, 12 of the world's 20 fastest-growing economies right now are on the African continent. East Africa alone is expanding at 6.4%, with Ethiopia clocking an extraordinary 9.8%.

Meanwhile, the U.S. is navigating a careful "soft landing" — resilient but decelerating. Consumer spending is slowing, inflation remains sticky at ~2.9% PCE, and the Fed is expected to cut rates only once or twice in 2026. Solid fundamentals, but a different growth story.

What's really turning heads? Africa's FDI surge. Foreign direct investment into Africa hit a record $97 billion in 2024 — a 75% jump year-over-year. That's the fastest FDI growth rate of any region globally. Remittances also rebounded strongly, reaching $104.6B and surpassing FDI as the largest external non-debt financing source for several nations.

The divergence is clear: mature economies are stabilizing while emerging markets — especially in Africa — are accelerating. For data professionals and investors, this shift creates enormous opportunities in analytics, infrastructure, and economic modeling across both regions.
