## Content

The LNG Super Cycle is here — and Africa is quietly becoming the world's next exploration frontier.

Global LNG capacity is set to jump from 357 MTPA in 2025 to over 520 MTPA by 2028 — a 46% surge. The US and Qatar account for two-thirds of new volume, with Golden Pass LNG and Qatar's North Field East leading the charge. But here's the tension: data centers are now competing with LNG exports for the same natural gas supply.

Now here's what most people aren't talking about — Africa.

Rystad Energy forecasts ~40% of all high-impact exploration wells drilled globally in 2026 will be in Africa. The Orange Basin, Gulf of Guinea, and East Africa are seeing unprecedented ultra-deepwater activity. TotalEnergies' Venus discovery offshore Namibia targets 160,000 bpd with FID expected this year. ENI reported a 500M barrel find in Angola and 1+ Tcf of new gas in Libya. Nigeria's reserves hit 215 Tcf.

OPEC+ confirmed a 206,000 b/d increase for May 2026, but involuntary cuts from Strait of Hormuz disruptions tell the real story. The IEA projects 3.8 mb/d potential oversupply once tensions ease — a critical data point for every energy analyst.

Carbon capture is scaling fast. ExxonMobil's Gulf Coast CCS network brings ~6.2 MTPA online in 2026, backed by $20B in low-carbon investment through 2030. Global CCUS investment hit $11.3B and the market targets $18.1B by 2032.

As a data analyst, this sector is a goldmine — from LNG price modeling to exploration risk assessment to emissions tracking. Companies like ExxonMobil, TotalEnergies, and Chevron are hiring data professionals with salaries reaching $450K+.

The energy transition isn't replacing oil & gas — it's transforming it. Data drives every decision.
