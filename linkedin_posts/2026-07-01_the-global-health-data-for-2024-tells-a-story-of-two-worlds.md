## Content

The global health data for 2024 tells a story of two worlds — and the gap is widening.

The US spends $15,474 per capita on healthcare. Sub-Saharan Africa averages around $135. That's a 100x difference — yet Africa carries the heaviest disease burden on the planet.

Here's what the numbers reveal:

📊 282 million malaria cases globally in 2024 — 95% of deaths concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa
🤱 70% of all global maternal deaths occur in Sub-Saharan Africa
💊 Only 55% of children living with HIV in the region are receiving treatment
🏥 Africa's Universal Health Coverage Index sits at 44/100 vs. a global benchmark of 71

Meanwhile, the US faces its own crisis: a projected shortage of 187,000+ physicians by 2037, and 171 million health records breached in 2023 alone. Digital health is booming ($88B market in 2025, heading to $266B by 2035), but cybersecurity and equity remain unresolved.

The data is clear: we're not just dealing with a health crisis — we're dealing with a data and resource allocation crisis. Better analytics, smarter investment, and cross-border collaboration can close these gaps faster than any single intervention.

For data professionals, health is one of the most impactful sectors to work in right now. The demand for health data analysts, epidemiologists, and digital health architects has never been higher — globally.
