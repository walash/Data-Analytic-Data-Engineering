## Content

304 million people. That’s how many international migrants are living outside their home country today — nearly double the figure from 1990.

Yet the data tells a more nuanced story than the headlines suggest.

📊 Here’s what the numbers actually show:

🌍 Global picture: Forced displacement hit a record 123.2 million in 2024 — 1 in every 67 people on earth. Conflict in Sudan alone displaced 14.3 million, nearly a third of its population.

🇺🇸 United States: The US remains the world’s #1 migrant destination, hosting 52.4 million international migrants. In 2024, it received 3.1 million new asylum applications — more than any other country. Policy is shifting sharply toward enforcement, but labor demand tells a different story: H-2B visa allocations rose to 64,716 for FY2026.

🌍 Africa: 64% of Sub-Saharan African migrants stay within the continent — migration here is largely regional, not the “exodus to the West” often portrayed. Remittances to the region are growing (1.6% in 2024, 2.1% in 2025), but still lag behind Latin America and South Asia.

💸 The economic angle: Global remittances to developing economies are projected to hit $690 billion in 2025 — exceeding both foreign direct investment AND official aid combined. Migration isn’t just a humanitarian issue; it’s a massive economic engine.

The data challenge? Policymakers often react to perception, not evidence. Data analysts working in this space have a real opportunity to shift that narrative.
