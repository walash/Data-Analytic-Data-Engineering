## Content

Migration is reshaping economies — and the data tells a story most people miss.

As of mid-2024, there are 304 million international migrants worldwide. The US alone hosts 52.4 million — roughly 17% of the global total, more than the entire African continent combined (29.2M). Yet Africa's migration story is largely internal: 86% of African migrants move within the continent, driven by labor demand and regional integration, not the Europe-bound narrative that dominates headlines.

What's striking is the financial dimension. Remittances to low- and middle-income countries hit a record $685 billion in 2024. Sub-Saharan Africa received $56 billion — a lifeline for millions of households. But here's the catch: sending money to Africa still costs ~8% on average, the highest globally. That's billions lost to transfer fees every year, money that could be building schools, funding businesses, and reducing poverty.

On the humanitarian side, forced displacement has reached an unprecedented 117.8 million people globally. Sudan alone has 9.1 million internally displaced persons — the world's largest IDP crisis. The DRC recorded 9.7 million new displacement movements in 2025. These aren't just statistics; they represent entire communities uprooted by conflict.

For data professionals, migration is one of the most complex, multi-dimensional datasets to work with — blending demographics, economics, geopolitics, and humanitarian indicators. The insights hidden in this data have real-world consequences.
