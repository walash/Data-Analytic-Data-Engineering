## Content

123.2 million people. That's the record number of forcibly displaced people on our planet as of end-2024 — more than the entire population of Mexico.

Yet the data tells a more nuanced story than headlines suggest.

📊 Here's what the numbers actually show:

 Global: 304 million international migrants now live outside their birth country — just 3.7% of the world's population. Migration is common, but not as universal as the debate implies.

🇺🇸 United States: Hosts 52.4 million migrants — 17% of the global total — making it the world's #1 destination by far. In FY2024, CBP recorded 2.9 million enforcement encounters, yet by December 2024, southwest border crossings had fallen 81% year-over-year following policy shifts.

🌍 Africa: Sub-Saharan Africa carries a disproportionate burden — 38.8 million internally displaced persons, nearly 1 in 3 of all forcibly displaced people globally. Sudan alone accounts for 14.3 million IDPs, the world's largest internal displacement crisis.

💸 The remittance gap is striking: African diaspora sent $54.3B home in 2024 — a lifeline for millions — but at an average cost of 7.73% vs. 6.35% globally. That "remittance penalty" costs African families an estimated $4.2B annually in fees alone.

And here's the brain drain reality: 46% of Africa's employed health workers reported intentions to migrate in 2024. The continent faces a projected shortfall of 5.85 million health workers by 2030.

Migration isn't just a policy debate — it's a data story about economic survival, displacement, and the cost of moving money across borders.
