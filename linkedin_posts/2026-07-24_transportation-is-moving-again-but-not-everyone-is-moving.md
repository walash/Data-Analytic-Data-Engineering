## Content

Transportation is moving again — but not everyone is moving at the same speed. 🚀

Global air traffic hit a record in 2024, with revenue passenger kilometers up 10.4% and the passenger load factor reaching an all-time high of 83.5%. The US alone screened 3.01 million travelers in a single day — a first in TSA history.

But here's what caught my attention as a data analyst:

📊 Africa's airlines grew even faster — RPKs up 13.2% YoY, outpacing the global rate. Yet Africa's load factor sits at 74.5%, nearly 9 points below the global benchmark. More demand, but still room to optimize.

🚢 Global container-port throughput reached 920 million TEUs (+6.9%), yet Suez and Panama Canal disruptions pushed Shanghai–Africa freight rates up 137–170%. Volume grew, but so did the cost of moving it.

⚡ The EV gap is striking: 17 million electric cars sold globally in 2024 vs. just ~11,000 in Africa — a 1,500× difference. Africa's infrastructure financing gap stands at $68–$108 billion annually, and 80% of infrastructure projects reportedly fail at the feasibility stage.

The data tells a clear story: demand is recovering faster than the systems built to support it — charging networks, logistics reliability, and infrastructure finance are the real bottlenecks.

For data professionals, transportation is one of the richest sectors to work in right now. The questions are complex, the stakes are high, and the analytics opportunities are enormous.
