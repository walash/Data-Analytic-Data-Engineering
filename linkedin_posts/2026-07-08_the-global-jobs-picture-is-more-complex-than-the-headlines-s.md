## Content

The global jobs picture is more complex than the headlines suggest. 📊

While the US unemployment rate sits at a seemingly comfortable 4.2%, dig deeper and you'll find 1.9 million Americans have been jobless for 27+ weeks — that's 27% of all unemployed people stuck in long-term joblessness. The labor market isn't broken, but it's quietly fracturing for a specific group.

Now zoom out to Africa, and the story shifts dramatically.

In Africa's MENA region, youth unemployment hits 24.5% — nearly 3x the US rate. In Sub-Saharan Africa, over 21.9% of young people are NEET (Not in Employment, Education, or Training). And here's the kicker: Africa's headline unemployment rate of ~6.7% actually looks better than the US on paper — but that's because 85%+ of employment is informal, meaning no contracts, no benefits, no stability.

The global picture? A projected 2.1 billion informal workers by 2026. The global youth unemployment rate hovers at 12.4%, and 20 out of every 100 young people worldwide are completely disconnected from work AND education.

What this tells us as data professionals:
→ Headline rates are lagging indicators — the real story is in participation, informality, and duration
→ Africa's job creation gap (needs 15M jobs/year, creates ~3M formal ones) is a data story waiting to be told
→ Long-term unemployment in mature economies signals structural mismatches, not just cyclical slowdowns

The data doesn't lie — but it does require context to speak clearly.
