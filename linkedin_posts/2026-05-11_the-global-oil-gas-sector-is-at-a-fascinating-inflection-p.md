## Content

The global Oil & Gas sector is at a fascinating inflection point — and the data tells a compelling story across three very different regions. 🌍

🇺🇸 In the US, crude production peaked at a record 13.6M b/d in 2025 before easing slightly to 13.5M b/d in 2026. Meanwhile, LNG exports are surging — from 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025 to a projected 18.6 Bcf/d by 2027. That's a 23% jump in just two years, cementing America's position as the world's top LNG supplier.

🌍 Africa is the one to watch. At ~7M b/d of combined oil & gas output, the continent is attracting serious capital — upstream capex is set to climb from $43B (2025) to $54B by 2030. Nigeria's LNG Train 7, TotalEnergies' Mozambique FID, Uganda's first oil, and Namibia's Venus discovery are all converging in 2026. This is a continent-wide energy renaissance.

🌐 Globally, demand is forecast at 106.5M b/d in 2026, driven almost entirely by non-OECD nations. Brent crude averaged $96/b for 2026 but spiked to $115/b in Q2 — a reminder that geopolitical risk premiums are very real. WTI is projected to reach $112/bbl within 12 months.

The data is clear: the energy transition is real, but hydrocarbons remain central to global growth — especially in emerging markets. Data analysts who understand energy flows, price dynamics, and regional investment patterns are increasingly valuable in this space.
