## Content

The energy investment story of 2026 isn't just about oil prices — it's about who can survive at what price, and where capital is actually flowing.
Here's what the data reveals:
💰 The Capital Shift Is Real — But Hydrocarbons Aren't Done
Global energy investment hits $3.4 trillion in 2026. Clean energy claims $2.2T vs. $1.2T for fossil fuels — nearly a 2:1 ratio. Yet oil and gas investment remains material, driven by energy security concerns and short-cycle projects in the US and Africa.
🇺🇸 US Shale: Scale Is Everything
The average WTI breakeven to drill a new well? $66/bbl. But large E&Ps (10,000+ b/d) need just $59/bbl — while small operators need $68/bbl. That $9/bbl gap is the difference between disciplined growth and margin pressure. US refineries are running at 97.4% utilization — near-maximum capacity with minimal buffer heading into fall maintenance season.
🌍 Africa: Massive Resources, Underserved by Capital
Africa produces 11.4 million boe/d yet receives only $41B in O&G investment — just 3.4% of global fossil-fuel spend. The Dangote refinery (650,000 b/d) is running at full capacity, and new projects in Angola, Zambia, and Algeria are targeting a $60-90B annual refined-product import bill. The downstream opportunity is enormous — but execution, not announcements, is what matters.
🌐 OPEC+ Complexity
Seven OPEC+ members agreed to a 188,000 b/d adjustment from September. But here's the nuance: much of the current supply gap is involuntary (outages, infrastructure damage) — not voluntary discipline. Distinguishing the two is critical for price forecasting.
The data analytics opportunity in energy has never been sharper — from shale breakeven modeling to refinery optimization to transition scenario planning.
