## Content

The global oil & gas market is being reshaped in real time — and the data tells a fascinating story. 🛢️📊

Brent crude hit $115/barrel in Q2 2026, driven by geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East that temporarily shut in nearly 10.8 million b/d of supply. Yet the US held firm — producing a record 13.5 million b/d, reinforcing its role as the world's anchor non-OPEC supplier.

Here's what the numbers reveal across three regions:

🇺🇸 United States: LNG exports are surging 23% — from 15.1 Bcf/d (2025) to a projected 18.6 Bcf/d by 2027. The US is now the world's top LNG exporter, reshaping global energy trade flows.

🌍 Africa: Capital expenditure in upstream oil & gas is set to jump 25.6% — from $43B (2025) to $54B by 2030. Nigeria, Angola, and deepwater projects are attracting renewed investor confidence as Europe diversifies away from Russian gas.

🌐 Global: Demand stands at 106.5 million b/d in 2026, with agency forecasts diverging sharply — EIA projects $95/bbl average while Goldman Sachs sees $85/bbl. The energy transition is slowing demand growth, but not stopping it.

The data analytics opportunity here is enormous — from price forecasting models to supply chain optimization and ESG reporting frameworks.
