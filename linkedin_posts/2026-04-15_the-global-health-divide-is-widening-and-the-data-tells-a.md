The global health divide is widening — and the data tells a stark story.

In 2024, the US spent $15,474 per person on healthcare — roughly 195x more than Sub-Saharan Africa's ~$79 per capita. Yet despite this spending, nearly 1 in 4 insured Americans is still considered underinsured, delaying care due to out-of-pocket costs.

Meanwhile, Africa is making real strides. Life expectancy has climbed from 55 years in 2000 to a projected 64.2 years in 2025-2026 — a 17% gain driven by HIV/AIDS control and child survival programs. But the continent still faces a health worker shortage of 6.1 million by 2030, and Sub-Saharan Africa accounts for ~70% of global maternal deaths.

Globally, the picture is mixed:
📊 The digital health market is projected at $380–420B in 2025, fueled by telehealth and AI adoption
📊 Global maternal mortality remains at 197 deaths per 100,000 live births — far from the SDG target of <70 by 2030
📊 Non-communicable diseases now dominate the global disease burden, straining health systems from Lagos to Los Angeles

The 2026 health story isn't just about innovation — it's about whether capital, workforce capacity, and access can scale fast enough to close these regional gaps.
