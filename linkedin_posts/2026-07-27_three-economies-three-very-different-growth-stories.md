## Content

Three economies. Three very different growth stories. 📊

The latest data paints a fascinating picture of global economic divergence in 2026 — and the numbers tell a story worth paying attention to.

🇺🇸 United States: Real GDP grew 2.2% in 2025, with Q1 2026 coming in at 2.1% annualized. Solid, but headline CPI has crept back up to 3.5% year-on-year as of June 2026 — a reminder that the "soft landing" still has turbulence ahead.

🌍 Africa: The continent is projected to grow at 4.3% in 2026 — more than double the US rate. FDI inflows surged 75% in 2024, and intra-African trade hit $192 billion. The challenge? Inflation still running at ~10.3%, and investment remains heavily concentrated in a handful of markets.

🌐 Global: The IMF's July 2026 update pegs world growth at 3.0%, with AI-linked investment providing a meaningful cushion. But merchandise trade growth is slowing sharply — from 4.6% in 2025 to a projected 1.9% in 2026. Geopolitical energy shocks are stalling disinflation.

The divergence is the story. Africa's growth momentum is real, but the financing gap for digital infrastructure alone stands at ~$14 billion annually. Meanwhile, the US faces renewed external balance pressure with a current account deficit widening to $226.8B in Q1 2026.

For data professionals, this is exactly the kind of multi-dimensional, cross-regional analysis that drives strategic decisions — from investment allocation to policy design.
