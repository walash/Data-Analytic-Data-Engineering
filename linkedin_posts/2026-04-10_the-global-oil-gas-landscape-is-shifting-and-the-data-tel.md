## Content

The global Oil & Gas landscape is shifting  and the data tells a compelling story.

🇺🇸 The US produced a record 13.2M barrels/day in 2024, with the Permian Basin alone accounting for 48% of output. Efficiency gains are so strong that production keeps rising even as active rigs dropped 5%. By 2025, output is forecast to hit 13.5M b/d.

 Africa's top producers — Nigeria (1.56M b/d), Libya (1.36M b/d), Algeria (1.18M b/d), and Angola (1.05M b/d) — collectively represent a significant share of global supply. Yet the continent's output remains fragmented and sensitive to geopolitical dynamics.

 Globally, upstream investment hit $600B in 2024 — the highest in a decade — before easing to ~$570B in 2025. The pivot? Capital is flowing toward natural gas and LNG, with LNG investment forecast at $171B in 2024 alone. The IEF estimates $4.3 trillion in cumulative investment will be needed by 2030 just to meet demand.

What's striking: US oil & gas workers earn an average of $135K/year — 77% above the national average. Specialized extraction roles average $215K. The sector remains one of the highest-paying in the world.

The energy transition is real, but so is the continued demand for hydrocarbons. The question isn't if — it's how fast, and who captures the value.
