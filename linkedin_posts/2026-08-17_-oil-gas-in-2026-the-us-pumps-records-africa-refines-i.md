🛢️ Oil & Gas in 2026: The US Pumps Records, Africa Refines Its Future
The global oil & gas sector is navigating a fascinating paradox — record production meets falling prices, while Africa quietly rewrites its energy story.
📊 What the data tells us:
🇺🇸 United States:
US crude output hit an all-time record of 13.6 mb/d in mid-2025, forecast to reach 14.0 mb/d by 2027 — while the number of producing wells has actually declined. Horizontal drilling efficiency is doing the heavy lifting. Brent crude softening to ~$65/b by 2027 squeezes margins even as volumes climb.
🌍 Africa:
Nigeria's Dangote Refinery — the world's largest single-train refinery at 650,000 bpd — stress-tested at 700,000 bpd with a long-term target of 1.4 million bpd. Africa historically spent $60–90B/year importing refined fuels. That equation is changing. TotalEnergies' Mozambique LNG has restarted construction (17.5 bcm/yr, first cargo 2029), and Africa's upstream CAPEX has hit $41B.
🌐 Global:
Clean energy investment now doubles fossil fuel spending ($2.2T vs $1.1T in 2025), yet global oil demand still grew 1.4 mb/d. The transition is real — but so is continued hydrocarbon demand. AI and data analytics are becoming mission-critical: from seismic processing to predictive maintenance.
The analyst's lens: Africa holds 532 Tcf of proven gas reserves but less than 1% of global data centers. Closing that infrastructure gap is where the next wave of energy analytics talent will be needed.
