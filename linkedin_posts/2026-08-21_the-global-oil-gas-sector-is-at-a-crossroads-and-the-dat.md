## Content

The global oil & gas sector is at a crossroads — and the data tells a striking story.

🇺🇸 The US produced 13.6 mb/d of crude in 2025 — a new record — while LNG exports surged to 15.1 Bcf/d, up from 11.9 Bcf/d in 2024. By 2030, the US is projected to supply ~1/3 of global LNG. That's not just energy dominance; it's a data infrastructure story: AI-driven drilling optimization and predictive maintenance are delivering these gains with fewer wells and fewer workers.

🌍 Africa's picture is more complex. Nigeria, Libya, Angola, and Algeria together produce ~4.92 mb/d — less than 5% of global supply — yet oil revenues remain the economic backbone of these nations. Nigeria's Dangote refinery hit 700,000 b/d in a June 2026 performance test, a landmark shift toward domestic value addition.

But here's the investment paradox: globally, clean energy investment outpaces fossil fuels 2:1 ($2.2T vs $1.1T). In Africa, that ratio is inverted — fossil fuel investment ($70B) still exceeds clean energy ($40B). Africa receives just ~1.8% of global clean energy capital despite holding 17% of the world's population.

📊 The data analytics opportunity in this sector is enormous — from seismic processing to supply chain optimization to energy transition modeling. Companies that master data will define the next decade of energy.
