## Content

Migration isn't just a policy debate — it's one of the most data-rich stories of our time. 📊

Here's what the numbers actually say for 2024-2025:

🌍 304 million people live outside their country of birth globally. The US alone hosts 52.4M — that's 17% of the world's entire migrant stock, nearly double Africa's 29.2M.

💸 Remittances tell a striking story: the US sends $103.2B outward annually (the world's largest single outflow), yet all of Sub-Saharan Africa receives just $54.6B in — barely half of what one country sends out. Global remittances to low- and middle-income countries hit $685B in 2024.

🔴 Africa's displacement crisis is largely invisible in global headlines: 31.7M internally displaced persons — ~27% of the world's 117.3M forcibly displaced — yet most of this movement never crosses a border.

📉 The US saw its most dramatic migration reversal in decades in 2025: SW border apprehensions dropped to 237K (FY2025), the lowest in over 50 years, driven by sweeping policy changes. Meanwhile, 45.8M new climate-driven displacements occurred globally in 2024 alone.

The data is clear: migration challenges are fundamentally different by region. What looks like a "border crisis" in one country is a humanitarian displacement emergency in another — and the remittance economy quietly underpins both.

As data professionals, we have a responsibility to tell these stories with precision, not politics.
