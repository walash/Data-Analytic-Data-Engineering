The global economy is holding steady in 2026  - but the real story is WHERE the growth is happening.

The IMF's latest World Economic Outlook projects global GDP growth at 3.3% for 2026, but beneath that headline number lies a fascinating divergence. Africa is emerging as the fastest-growing region globally, with the African Development Bank forecasting 4.3% average growth 

Here's what the data tells us:

The US economy is projected to expand 2.4% in 2026, fueled by AI-driven capital expenditure exceeding $200B and tax cuts from the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act." Goldman Sachs sees it hitting 2.8% on a full-year basis 

India remains a powerhouse at 6.4%, while China moderates to 4.5% as structural headwinds persist.

But Africa's story is the one to watch. East Africa leads at 5.8% growth, driven by Ethiopia and Kenya's structural reforms and renewable energy expansion. Nigeria is set to overtake South Africa as Africa's top contributor to global growth 

The risks? Trade tensions, AI bubble concerns, and debt burdens (Africa's public debt-to-GDP hit 63%). But the structural tailwinds
