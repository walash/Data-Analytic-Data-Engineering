## Content

The global oil & gas market is being reshaped in real time — and the data tells a fascinating story.

A geopolitical supply shock has removed 11+ million barrels/day from global markets, sending Brent crude from $69/b in 2025 to a projected $95/b in 2026 — a 38% surge in a single year.

Here's what the numbers reveal across regions:

🇺🇸 United States
→ Crude production hit 13.6M b/d in 2025 (record high), rising to 13.7M b/d in 2026
→ Net exports peaked at 5.8M b/d in April 2026 — the US is now the world's critical swing supplier
→ LNG exports surging: 15.1 BCF/day (2025) → 17.2 BCF/day (2026)

🌍 Africa
→ Nigeria, Libya, Algeria & Angola collectively produce ~5.4M b/d
→ Africa spent $120B importing fuel in 2024 — but Nigeria's Dangote Refinery (650K bpd) is changing that equation
→ Algeria's $60B investment plan (2025-29) signals serious upstream ambition
→ Africa attracts only 2% of global clean energy investment — a massive gap and opportunity

🌐 Global
→ OECD inventories projected to hit lowest levels since 2003
→ Clean energy captures $2.2T of $3.3T global energy investment in 2025
→ Prices expected to normalize to $79/b by 2027 — IF the Hormuz crisis resolves

The divergence is clear: developed markets are accelerating the energy transition while African producers are doubling down on gas as a bridge fuel. Both strategies make sense given their contexts.

What does this mean for data professionals? Energy companies are investing heavily in analytics to optimize production, predict price movements, and model transition scenarios.
