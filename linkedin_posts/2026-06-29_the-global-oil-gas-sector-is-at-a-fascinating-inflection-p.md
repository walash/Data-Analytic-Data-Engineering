## Content

The global Oil & Gas sector is at a fascinating inflection point — and the data tells a compelling story across three very different fronts.

🇺🇸 United States: Record production, record exports
The US hit 13.7M barrels/day of crude output in 2026 and became the first country ever to export 100M+ metric tons of LNG in a single year (2025). America isn't just producing energy — it's reshaping global supply chains.

🌍 Africa: Massive resources, massive gap
Africa produces 11.4M boe/day of hydrocarbons, yet spent $120 billion importing refined products in 2024. The Dangote Refinery (650,000 b/d capacity) is a game-changer — it's already exporting gasoline to the US and Asia. But with demand projected to exceed 6M b/d by 2050, the continent still has a long road ahead on downstream infrastructure.

🌐 Global: Geopolitics > Fundamentals
When the Strait of Hormuz was disrupted in early 2026, ~20M b/d of supply went offline — more than the entire US production. Brent spiked to $119/barrel. This is the new reality: supply risk, not demand growth, is driving price action. Global demand growth is a modest 1.4M b/d in 2026, yet prices remain elevated.

The structural story? A looming supply glut by 2030 (8M+ b/d spare capacity) vs. short-term geopolitical volatility. Data analysts and energy strategists have never been more essential.
