## Content

304 million people now live outside their country of birth. That's nearly 4% of humanity — and the data tells a story beyond the headlines.

Here's what the numbers actually show:

🌍 Global picture: The international migrant stock doubled from 154M in 1990 to 304M in 2024. The US alone hosts 52.4M migrants — 17% of the global total — the world's #1 destination by a wide margin.

🌐 Africa's paradox: The continent hosts 36% of the world's forcibly displaced people (42.7M out of 117.8M globally), yet receives just 8.2% of global remittance flows ($56B of $685B). Africa carries a disproportionate humanitarian burden while receiving far less economic support.

💸 The remittance tax: Sub-Saharan Africa pays 8.37% to send $200 home — 31% above the global average of 6.4%. That's a structural penalty on the world's most vulnerable migrant workers, costing billions in lost household income annually.

🇺🇸 US enforcement shift: FY2025 southwest border apprehensions fell to 237,538 — the lowest since 1970. A 95% drop in March 2025 vs. March 2024 signals one of the sharpest enforcement pivots in modern US immigration history.

🔴 Sudan's crisis: 9.1M internally displaced + 2.8M refugees — nearly 12M people uprooted from one country, representing ~10% of all global forced displacement.

The data is clear: migration isn't slowing, but the systems meant to support migrants — from remittance infrastructure to humanitarian funding — are struggling to keep pace.
