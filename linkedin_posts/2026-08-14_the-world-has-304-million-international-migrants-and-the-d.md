## Content

The world has 304 million international migrants — and the data tells a story of sharp divergence, not a single trend.

Here's what the numbers reveal across the US, Africa, and globally:

🌍 GLOBAL PICTURE
The international migrant stock hit 304 million at mid-2024 (3.7% of world population), up from just 154 million in 1990. Yet forced displacement — a separate measure — actually declined from 123.2M at end-2024 to 117.8M at end-2025. Progress, but 117 million people still forcibly displaced is a crisis by any measure.

🇺🇸 UNITED STATES
US net international migration dropped 52% — from 2.7M in 2024 to 1.3M in 2025. Southwest border apprehensions fell to 237,538 in FY2025, the lowest since 1970. Meanwhile, foreign-born workers now make up 19.1% of the US civilian labor force, contributing across every sector of the economy.

🌍 AFRICA
Africa's migration story is largely intraregional — 64% of sub-Saharan-born migrants live within sub-Saharan Africa itself. But the continent faces a dual challenge: Sudan alone accounts for 9.1M internally displaced people and 2.8M refugees abroad. And remittances — $54B flowing into sub-Saharan Africa in 2023 — remain a critical economic lifeline, yet sending $200 still costs 8.46% in fees vs. the 3% SDG target.

💡 THE DATA ANALYST'S LENS
Migration data is notoriously fragmented — stock vs. flow, documented vs. undocumented, enforcement events vs. net migration. The $685B in remittances to low/middle-income countries exceeds both foreign aid and FDI combined. That's the real story hiding in plain sight.
