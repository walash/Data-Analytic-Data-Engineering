## Content

The Intel vs AMD debate isn't what it used to be. Here's what the data actually shows in 2025-2026:

AMD's comeback is one of tech's greatest turnarounds. From 18% desktop share in 2020 to 32.2% in Q2 2025, and server revenue share hitting a record 41% -- AMD has reshaped the competitive landscape. Intel went from outselling AMD 9:1 to just 2:1.

PERFORMANCE DATA (Benchmarked):
- Gaming: AMD's 3D V-Cache (R7 9800X3D) leads Intel's i9-285K by 10-17% in AAA titles at 1080p
- Multi-core: Ryzen 9 9950X wins in Blender, V-Ray (17% faster), CoronaRender (15% faster)
- Single-core: Intel Core Ultra 9 285K holds a slight edge in single-threaded workloads
- Efficiency: AMD's R7 9800X3D delivers 140 FPS in Cyberpunk at 125W vs Intel's 136 FPS at 210W
- Servers: AMD EPYC dominates performance-per-watt, projected to reach 50% market share by 2026

PRICE/PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS:
AMD consistently delivers more cores per dollar across most segments. Intel's Arrow Lake removed Hyper-Threading but moved to TSMC 3nm for better efficiency. AMD's AM5 socket supports multiple CPU generations, while Intel's LGA 1851 appears to be single-generation -- a significant factor for long-term value.

THE VERDICT: It genuinely depends on YOUR specific use case.
Choose AMD for: Gaming, 3D rendering, budget builds, long-term upgrades, data centers
Choose Intel for: Adobe Creative Suite (Quick Sync), AI/NPU workloads, eSports, laptop battery life

The real winner? Consumers. Healthy competition drives innovation, better products, and lower prices every generation.
