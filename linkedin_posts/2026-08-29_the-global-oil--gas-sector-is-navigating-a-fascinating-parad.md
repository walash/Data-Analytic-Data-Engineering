## Content

The global oil & gas sector is navigating a fascinating paradox: record US supply, a massive LNG buildout in Africa, and a looming capacity surplus by 2030 — all simultaneously.

Here's what the data shows:

🇺🇸 United States — Record Output, Lean Workforce
US crude production hit 13.6 million b/d in 2025 (a new record), forecast to reach 13.8M b/d in 2026. LNG exports surged to 15.1 Bcf/d in 2025, projected at 17.4 Bcf/d this year. The direct extraction workforce? Just ~115,000 people — with STEM roles at 18.5% of jobs vs. 6.7% across all US industries. Data analytics and automation are redefining what "working in oil & gas" means.

🌍 Africa — Big Projects, Bigger Financing Gap
Africa exported 5.04 million b/d of crude in 2025, with $103B+ in LNG terminal capex in development. Angola's Quiluma gas field came online in March 2026. Nigeria's Dangote refinery hit 700,000 b/d in June. Yet Africa received just 2% of global clean energy investment despite 20% of the world's population — and needs $190B+/year through 2030 to close the energy access gap.

🌐 Global — Disruption Now, Surplus Later?
Global upstream investment is ~$570B in 2025, down ~4% YoY. The IEA projects 114.7 mb/d of capacity by 2030 against demand near 105.5 mb/d — a potential 9.2 mb/d surplus. Meanwhile, WTI hit $84/b in August 2026 amid supply disruptions. Short-term and long-term signals are pointing in opposite directions.

The data analytics opportunity here is enormous — from production optimization to investment risk modeling to energy transition scenario planning.
