Transportation is booming — but the data tells a more complicated story than the headlines suggest.

In 2024, global airline traffic hit a record, growing 10.4% in revenue passenger kilometers and finishing 3.8% above pre-pandemic 2019 levels. Africa outpaced the world at 13.2% RPK growth. Yet Africa's load factor (74.5%) still trails the global record of 83.5% — meaning demand is surging faster than airlines can efficiently fill seats.

On freight: global container-port throughput reached 920 million TEUs (+6.9%), but Suez and Panama Canal disruptions forced longer voyages. The result? Shanghai–South Africa freight rates nearly tripled to $5,426/TEU by mid-2024. More volume, higher cost — efficiency didn't keep pace with demand.

The EV story is where the divergence gets dramatic:
→ Global: 17M+ electric cars sold in 2024 (>20% market share)
→ US: 5.7M light-duty EVs registered; DC fast-charging ports growing 8.2% per quarter
→ Africa: ~11,000 EV sales in 2024 (<1% market share)

That's a 1,500× scale gap. Africa's growth rate is impressive — sales more than doubled — but the absolute numbers reveal a market still in its earliest innings.

Meanwhile, Africa faces a $68–$108B annual infrastructure financing gap, with 80% of projects reportedly failing at the feasibility stage. The continent moves 80% of its freight and 90% of passengers by road, yet only 53% of roads are paved.

The analytical takeaway: demand indicators are rising faster than the enabling systems — charging networks, logistics reliability, and infrastructure finance — in every region, but especially in Africa.
