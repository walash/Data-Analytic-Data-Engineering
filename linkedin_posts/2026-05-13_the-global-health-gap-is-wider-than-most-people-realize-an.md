The global health gap is wider than most people realize — and the data tells a striking story.

The US spends $15,474 per person on healthcare annually (18% of GDP). Africa averages just ~$135. Yet the life expectancy gap? Only 13 years. That's a 115x spending difference for a fraction of the outcome gain.

Here's what the numbers reveal across US, Africa, and the world:

🇺🇸 United States: Leading in digital health ($88B market in 2025), with 96% EHR adoption in hospitals. But facing a looming crisis — projected shortages of 141,000+ physicians and 109,000+ nurses by 2038.

🌍 Africa: Average life expectancy of 64.2 years masks a 22-year gap within the continent itself — Tunisia at 76.9 years vs. Nigeria at 54.8. The difference? GDP per capita, governance, and health investment.

🌐 Global: Non-communicable diseases now cause 74% of all deaths worldwide — 43.8M annually — projected to hit 75.5M by 2050. Meanwhile, the world faces an 11 million health worker shortage by 2030.

The paradox is clear: the most tech-advanced health system in the world is running out of workers, while the regions with the greatest need have the fewest resources to train them.

Data analytics is increasingly central to solving this — from predictive workforce planning to disease surveillance and resource allocation across low-income settings.
