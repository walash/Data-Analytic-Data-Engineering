## Content

The global unemployment story is more complex than a single headline number.

The ILO projects global unemployment at 4.9% in 2025 — stable on the surface. But dig deeper and the picture shifts dramatically.

🇺🇸 In the US, unemployment sits at 4.3% (March 2026), with 178,000 nonfarm jobs added last month. Wages are up 3.5% year-over-year. Solid numbers — yet youth unemployment tells a different story at 13.7%.

🌍 Across Africa, the contrast is stark. Nigeria: 4.9%. Kenya: 5.4%. Ethiopia: 3.4%. But South Africa: 31.4%. The continent-wide average of ~10.3% masks enormous variation — and a structural challenge: 10 million young Africans enter the labor market annually, but only 3 million formal jobs are created. 90% of employed youth work in the informal sector.

🌐 Globally, youth unemployment is projected to hit a 15-year low of 12.8% in 2025 — progress worth celebrating. But the "jobs gap" (those who want work but aren't actively searching) still stands at 9% worldwide.

The real story isn't just about who's unemployed — it's about the quality of work, the skills mismatch, and whether formal economies are growing fast enough to absorb the next generation.

Data analytics has a critical role here: from labor market modeling to workforce planning, the insights we surface shape policy and investment decisions that affect millions.
