🏥 THE $13,800 QUESTION: Why does the world's biggest healthcare spender rank LAST among peers? A data analyst's dive into global health ROI (2026).

I analyzed spend vs life expectancy across 21 countries. More money ≠ more life.

📊 KEY FINDINGS
• Pearson r (linear) = 0.56 | Log r = 0.85
• Spend explains ~73% of variance — but only up to ~$4,000/capita. After that, returns collapse.

💸 THE US PARADOX
USA: $13,800/capita — 41% more than Switzerland, 155% more than the UK — yet Americans live 77.8 yrs vs Japan's 84.8 (-7 years). Residuals show US underperforms its spend by 5.2 yrs. A ~$4T/yr efficiency gap.

🏆 EFFICIENCY LEADERS (life-yrs per $1,000)
• Costa Rica — 81.3 yrs at $1,600 (the true winner)
• Japan — 84.8 yrs at $5,400 (best developed-world ROI)
• Singapore & S. Korea — universal + prevention
• India — 70.8 yrs at $320 (steepest marginal ROI)

🌍 REGIONAL SIGNAL
• Europe: tight cluster 81–84 yrs at $4k–$10k (equity pays)
• Asia: top positive outliers
• LATAM: Costa Rica > USA on outcomes at 1/8 the cost
• Africa: disease burden & access gaps, not just $$$

📈 2020–2026 TRENDS
• US spend ↑16%, life exp +0.8 yr
• Japan spend ↑15%, +0.2 yr (saturation)
• India spend ↑52%, +1.1 yr

🔑 WHY SPEND ≠ OUTCOMES
1. US admin overhead: 25–30% of spend
2. Fee-for-service vs capitation
3. Inequality & access
4. Lifestyle (obesity, diet)
5. Primary-care underinvestment

📌 TAKEAWAY
Universal coverage + primary care + prevention beat raw spending. The next trillion in US healthcare should target equity and chronic disease, not more tech.
