## Content

The global Oil & Gas sector is navigating a fascinating paradox — record production alongside accelerating energy transition capital.

Here's what the data tells us:

🇺🇸 US: Crude oil production hit an all-time high of 13.6M barrels/day in 2025, with forecasts pointing to 13.8M b/d in 2026. Remarkably, this was achieved with FEWER producing wells — horizontal drilling efficiency is doing the heavy lifting. Average wages in extraction? $122,890/year, making it one of America's highest-paying industries.

🌍 Africa: Nigeria's Dangote Refinery — the world's largest single-train refinery at 650,000 bpd nameplate capacity — has now stress-tested at 700,000 bpd and has a long-term target of 1.4M bpd. This is a structural game-changer for a continent that was spending $60–90B annually on imported refined fuels. Meanwhile, TotalEnergies' Mozambique LNG project (17.5 bcm/yr) resumed construction in early 2026 after force majeure.

🌐 Global: Total energy investment hit $3.3 trillion in 2025 — but clean energy ($2.2T) now outpaces fossil fuels ($1.1T) by 2:1. Africa receives less than 2% of global clean energy investment despite holding 20% of the world's population. That gap is both a challenge and an opportunity for data-driven capital allocation.

The AI angle is real too: 23% of Nigerian oil & gas firms have adopted AI tools, and predictive maintenance + seismic analytics are cutting drilling costs globally. Data professionals in this sector are increasingly mission-critical.
