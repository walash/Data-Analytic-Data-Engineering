## Content

The global oil & gas market is at a fascinating inflection point — and the data tells a story easy to miss if you're only watching prices.

In 2024, world crude production fell for the first time since 2020, dropping to 72.58 mb/d. Yet global oil demand climbed to 103.84 mb/d — a 1.5% increase. Brent averaged $81/barrel in 2024 and is now projected to spike to $87 in 2026, driven by Strait of Hormuz supply constraints.

Here's what stands out across three regions:

🇺🇸 United States: The US hit record production — 13.2 mb/d in 2024, forecast to reach 13.8 mb/d in 2026. But the real story is LNG. Exports surged from 11.9 Bcf/d in 2024 to 17.4 Bcf/d in H1 2026 — a 46% jump in two years. The US now supplies 26% of global LNG trade, with Europe absorbing 68% of those exports.

🌍 Africa: Nigeria averaged 1.58 mb/d in 2024 — but that's only 67% of its technical capacity. The 650,000 b/d Dangote refinery came online commercially, reshaping regional product economics. Angola (~1.03 mb/d), Libya (~1.41 mb/d), and Algeria (~0.94 mb/d) each navigate different constraints from aging fields to institutional risk.

🌐 Global outlook: Production capacity is projected to reach 114.7 mb/d by 2030, while demand plateaus around 105.5 mb/d. That ~9 mb/d cushion will intensify competition and pressure OPEC+ discipline. EVs alone are forecast to displace 5.4 mb/d by 2030.

Of the $3.3 trillion in global energy investment in 2025, $2.2 trillion went to clean energy vs. ~$570 billion upstream O&G. The transition isn't replacing oil overnight — but it's already reshaping where capital flows.
