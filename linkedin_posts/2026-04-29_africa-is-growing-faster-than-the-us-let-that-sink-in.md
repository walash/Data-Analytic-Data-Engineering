## Content

Africa is growing faster than the US. Let that sink in.

In 2024, Sub-Saharan Africa posted GDP growth of 3.5% — outpacing the United States at 1.7% and matching the global average of 3.2%. By 2025, Africa’s growth is projected to accelerate to 3.7%, while global growth holds flat.

Yet the story is more nuanced than the headline numbers suggest.

Global trade hit a record $33 trillion in 2024, driven largely by a 7% surge in services. But FDI flows to developing economies fell for the second consecutive year — meaning capital isn’t following the growth story in Africa fast enough.

For the US, the challenge is different: strong consumption is being offset by 4.2% inflation (OECD forecast), fiscal sustainability concerns flagged by the IMF, and policy uncertainty rippling through global value chains.

What does this mean for data professionals?

The divergence between regions creates massive demand for analysts who can translate macro signals into business strategy — whether you’re advising on market entry in Lagos, modeling risk in New York, or tracking FDI flows across emerging markets.

The data is clear: economic gravity is shifting. The question is whether organizations are building the analytical capacity to act on it.
