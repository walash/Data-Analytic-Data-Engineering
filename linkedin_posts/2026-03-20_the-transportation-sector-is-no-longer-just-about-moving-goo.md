## Content

The transportation sector is no longer just about moving goods from A to B — it's becoming one of the most data-intensive industries on the planet.
Here's what the numbers are telling us in 2026:
The global transportation analytics market is projected to hit $26.7B this year, racing toward $89.4B by 2033 at an 18.9% CAGR. In the US alone, the logistics market is valued at $516.5B, with 3PL providers capturing over 61% of market share. Meanwhile, autonomous vehicle revenue is expected to reach $626.9B globally, with Waymo now operating 2,500+ robotaxis across 10 US cities.
But here's the story that doesn't get enough attention — Africa.
The continent faces a $170B annual infrastructure financing gap, yet transformative projects are underway. The Abidjan-Lagos Corridor, a 1,028 km transnational motorway connecting five West African nations, has attracted $15.6B in investment interest. South Africa just announced a R1-trillion ($53B) public infrastructure program for 2025/26. The AfCFTA needs $32B annually in transport and logistics infrastructure to unlock a single market of 1.3 billion people.
As a data analyst, what excites me most is the shift from descriptive to predictive analytics in this space. AI-driven fleet management now focuses 65% of queries on vehicle performance and fuel efficiency. Madrid Metro's AI maintenance scheduling cut rolling stock failures by 30%. Autonomous trucks are saving FedEx over $200M annually while Amazon has slashed carbon emissions by 35% through their deployment.
The talent demand is massive — and the skills gap is real. Organizations need professionals who can bridge transportation domain expertise with advanced analytics capabilities.
