## Content

The data gap between US and African oil production is wider than most people realize — and AI might be the bridge.

The US is forecast to produce 13.8 million barrels/day in 2026. Nigeria, Libya, Angola, and Algeria combined? About 4.8 million b/d. Africa holds ~7.5% of global proven reserves yet contributes only ~5% of global supply. That's not just a production gap — it's an infrastructure, investment, and technology gap.

A few numbers that tell the story:

📊 US crude output: 13.8M b/d (2026 forecast)
📊 Nigeria leads Africa at 1.5M b/d — just 11% of US output
📊 Africa attracts only 3.3% of global energy investment
📊 Global clean energy now gets $2 for every $1 going to fossil fuels

Meanwhile, AI is reshaping the entire value chain. 92% of oil & gas enterprises globally are investing in AI. Predictive maintenance alone accounts for 32% of AI spend — reducing unplanned refinery downtime by up to 25%. AI-powered seismic interpretation has improved exploration success rates by 17%.

For African producers, this is both a challenge and an opportunity. Nigeria's Dangote refinery (650,000 b/d capacity) is a landmark step toward energy self-sufficiency. But scaling AI adoption — where North America currently captures 40% of the market — requires deliberate investment in data infrastructure and local talent.

The next competitive edge in oil & gas won't just come from what's underground. It'll come from the data layer on top.
