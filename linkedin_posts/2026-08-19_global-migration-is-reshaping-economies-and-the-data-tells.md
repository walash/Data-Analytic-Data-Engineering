## Content

Global migration is reshaping economies — and the data tells a story most headlines miss.

304 million people now live outside their country of birth. That's 3.7% of humanity on the move — up from 275M just four years ago. The US hosts 52.4M of them, making it the world's largest migrant destination. Africa hosts 29.2M, but here's what's striking: 86% of Africa's migrants come from within Africa itself. South-South migration is the dominant story, not the North-bound narrative we often hear.

The US picture shifted dramatically in 2025. Net migration fell 51.9% — from 2.7M in 2024 to just 1.3M in 2025. Southwest border apprehensions dropped to 237,538 in FY2025, the lowest since 1970. Policy-driven contraction at scale.

Meanwhile, forced displacement globally declined for the first time in years — from 123.2M at end-2024 to 117.8M at end-2025. Progress, but 41.6M refugees and 68.7M internally displaced people remain. Sudan alone accounts for 9.1M IDPs and 2.8M refugees abroad.

The remittance story is equally powerful. $685B flowed to low- and middle-income countries in 2024 — exceeding foreign direct investment and official development aid combined. Sub-Saharan Africa received $56B, yet sending $200 still costs 8.46% on average, nearly 3x the UN's 3% SDG target. Every percentage point in fees is money taken from families.

Data analytics is critical here — tracking corridors, modeling displacement risk, optimizing remittance flows, and informing policy. The organizations doing this work are shaping outcomes for millions.
