## Content

Africa is growing at twice the US rate — yet inflation is the silent tax eating those gains.

The latest IMF and World Bank data tell a fascinating story of economic divergence in 2025–2026:

📊 GDP Growth Rates:
• Africa: 4.2% (2025) → 4.3% (2026 forecast)
• Global: 3.0% (2026 IMF forecast)
• United States: 2.2% (2025) → 2.1% annualized in Q1 2026

Africa is outpacing both the US and the global average — driven by resilient private consumption, accommodative monetary conditions, and a surge in FDI that jumped 75% in 2024.

But here's the catch: Africa's inflation sits at 13.7% in 2025, compared to 3.5% in the US and 4.7% globally. Strong headline growth doesn't automatically translate to improved living standards when prices are rising that fast.

What's driving Africa's momentum?
→ FDI inflows up 75% in 2024 (though Egypt's Ras El-Hekma megaproject skews the headline)
→ Intra-African trade reached $192B in 2023, up 7.2%
→ Digital services exports hit $36.7B — 4x the 2005 level
→ Sub-Saharan Africa on track for 4.1% growth in 2026 (World Bank)

Meanwhile, the US current account deficit widened to $226.8B in Q1 2026 (2.9% of GDP), and global merchandise trade growth is forecast to slow from 4.6% in 2025 to just 1.9% in 2026.

The data analytics opportunity here is enormous. Modeling growth divergence, inflation-adjusted returns, and FDI concentration risk across African markets is exactly the kind of work that creates real strategic value.
