## Content

The unemployment rate tells you who's looking. It doesn't tell you who's struggling.

Here's what the data actually shows for 2025-2026 👇

🇺🇸 The US headline rate sits at 4.3% — historically low. But dig deeper: 27.5% of unemployed Americans have been jobless for 27+ weeks. That's 2 million people stuck in long-term unemployment, up 524,000 in just one year. The financial sector alone shed 107,000 jobs since May 2025.

🌍 In North Africa, 1 in 3 young job-seekers can't find work — a youth unemployment rate of 33.3%. In Sub-Saharan Africa, 87.6% of employment is informal, and 21.8% of youth are NEET (Not in Employment, Education, or Training). Low headline rates here mask a crisis of economic exclusion, not stability.

🌐 Globally, the ILO's "jobs gap" — those unemployed PLUS those who want work but stopped looking — hit 407 million people in 2025. Meanwhile, 63% of employers say they can't find workers with the right skills. The problem isn't just jobs. It's the mismatch between the jobs that exist and the skills people have.

The data analytics opportunity here is massive. Every one of these trends — duration, informality, skills gaps — is a dataset waiting to be modeled, visualized, and turned into policy or business strategy.
